What this is

It's a bingo caller that lives on a screen instead of in a cage of plastic balls. You run it on a laptop or a desktop PC, plug that machine into your TV or a monitor with an HDMI cable, and the whole room can see the board.

You stay in charge of the pace. Press the button (or tap the spacebar) and it pulls the next number, lights it up on the master board, and puts it in big type in the corner. Then you read it out, the way it's always been done.

It's deliberately silent. Computers can read numbers aloud, but the voices still sound like computers, and a bingo hall deserves better than that. The code for it is in there and switched off — the day the voices are good enough to be worth listening to, it's a one-line change. Until then, the room gets a person.

It runs entirely in your browser. No installer, no login, no internet connection needed once the page is open.

About the free part

“Organizations usually have to pay for something like this for them to make money — not us, not here.” — Jake, who built it

Bingo is how a lot of groups keep the lights on. The hall, the ladder truck, the food pantry, the class trip. And the software that runs the game usually wants a cut of that — a license, a subscription, a per-seat fee, money coming out of the same pot that was supposed to go to the cause.

That always seemed backwards. So this one doesn't cost anything. There's no price, no trial that ends, no account to make, no email to hand over, no feature locked behind a button that turns on later. Save it, use it every week for the next ten years, and pass it along to the hall down the road if it helps them too.

If it makes your night easier, that's the whole return.

How to use it

  1. Put it on the big screen

    Run an HDMI cable from your laptop or PC to the TV or monitor. Mirroring the display works fine. Extending it works better — then you can drag the caller window onto the TV and keep your own desktop free.

  2. Go fullscreen

    Open the caller, click CLICK TO START, then hit FULL SCREEN in the sidebar. The board fills the whole TV. The screen won't go to sleep on you mid-game.

  3. Pick your pattern

    CHOOSE GAME gives you 17 patterns — full card, X, T, L, four corners, postage stamp, heart, kite, block of six, and more — plus a blank grid you can paint cell by cell for house rules. Confirm it and a big NOW PLAYING screen shows the room what they're playing for.

  4. Call the numbers

    Press START, then the spacebar for each draw. The Pace Delay slider (0–15 seconds) keeps you from getting ahead of the room. RESET shuffles a fresh game whenever you're ready for the next one.

What's in it

  • Full master board

    All 75 numbers on screen at once, in color-coded B/I/N/G/O rows. Nobody has to ask what's been called.

  • Big last-call display

    The number that was just drawn, in type you can read from the back of the hall.

  • 17 patterns + a custom painter

    Presets for the usual games, and a blank card for whatever your house plays.

  • Rotating pattern preview

    Shows every legal orientation of a shape, so nobody argues about whether a sideways L counts.

  • Pace control

    A 0–15 second delay between calls so the room can keep up with you.

  • Close a letter

    Click a B/I/N/G/O row header to take that letter out of the draw for specialty games.

  • Fullscreen + no sleep

    Fills the TV and keeps the screen awake through a long session.

  • Works offline

    One file, zero outside dependencies. Once it's open, you can unplug the internet.

  • Nothing to install

    It opens in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari like any web page. No download, no setup, no account.

  • Silent on purpose

    No robot voice talking over you. The screen tracks the game; the calling stays human.

Straight talk about what it doesn't do

  • It does not print bingo cards. You bring your own cards and dabbers, same as always.
  • It does not verify a winner. When somebody shouts, you check their card against the board by eye, the way it's always been done.
  • It doesn't track players, money, or payouts. It calls numbers. That's the job.
  • It's built for a computer or TV, not a phone. A phone screen can't hold 75 numbers at a readable size.
  • It's not a licensed gaming system. If your state or organization has rules about what equipment a charitable game can use, check those first — that part's on you.
