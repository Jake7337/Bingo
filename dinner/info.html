What it actually does

You hit one button. It names one ordinary dinner — the kind that really shows up on a table on a Tuesday. Don't like it? Hit it again. That's the app.

What it is not

It's not a recipe app. There's no shopping list, no meal planner, no calorie count, no account, no ads, nothing to sign up for. It doesn't ask what's in your fridge or how much time you've got, because at 5 o'clock nobody wants to answer questions — they want somebody else to decide.

There are 105 dinners in it, all plain everyday food: pot roast, chicken and noodles, haluski, tacos, ham pot pie, tater tot casserole, breakfast for dinner, leftovers. Nothing you'd need to go shopping for a special ingredient to make.

Small things that matter

  • It won't repeat itself. Nothing from the last dozen taps comes back around, so it never feels stuck on tacos.
  • It works offline. Download the file, open it on a phone or laptop with no internet, and it still works. Forever.
  • Nothing is saved or sent. No account, no tracking, no server. It doesn't know who you are and never will.
  • You can edit the list. Open the downloaded file in Notepad, find the list of dinners near the bottom, and add your family's. One per line.

About the free part

It's free because it should be. No trial, no upgrade, no catch. If it settles one argument at supper time it did its job.
